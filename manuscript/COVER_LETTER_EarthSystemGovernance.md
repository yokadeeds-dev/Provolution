I am pleased to submit the manuscript *"Probatio Systemica & Provolution: A Systematic, Quantified Framework for Climate Transformation"* for consideration in *Earth System Governance*.

**Why this paper, why this journal.**
Earth System Governance is explicitly dedicated to the study of institutional architectures, governance mechanisms, and their capacity to address planetary-scale challenges. This manuscript contributes directly to that agenda by introducing a mathematically grounded verification framework — Probatio Systemica — and its climate-specific application, Provolution, which provides a governance-compatible tool for the systematic prioritization, validation, and coordination of climate measures across domains. The framework operationalizes three governance-relevant properties — sufficiency, efficiency, and consistency — as a unified, reproducible scoring instrument (the SEC score), addressing a structural gap that Earth System Governance scholars have repeatedly identified: the absence of cross-domain verification standards for climate action portfolios.

**Core contribution.**
Applied to 40 quantified climate measures spanning ten domains (governance, circular economy, energy, food, education, technology, monitoring, meta-framework, mobility, and construction), the framework yields an average SEC score of 0.914 (range 0.88–1.00) and an aggregate CO₂ mitigation potential of −58.0 Gt/year — exceeding current global emissions of 55 Gt/year (105%) and providing net-negative potential through active carbon sequestration. Crucially, consistency across measures is treated as a first-class verification criterion, not a secondary consideration. This directly addresses the coordination failure that characterizes current nationally determined contributions under the Paris Agreement.

**Methodological transparency.**
The framework is explicitly falsifiable through three operationalized falsification conditions. All data, formulas, application templates, and scoring documentation are publicly available under CC0 1.0 (public domain) at https://github.com/yokadeeds-dev/Provolution, enabling independent replication and extension by the research community.

**Independent research.**
This work is conducted as independent research. The absence of institutional affiliation reflects a deliberate commitment to framework-neutral, open-science methodology: no funding constraints, no institutional paradigm, and full CC0 release ensure that the framework serves the broadest possible user community — including researchers and practitioners in the Global South, who face the highest climate risks under the most severe resource constraints.
